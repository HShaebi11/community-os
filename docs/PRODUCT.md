# Product

**Community OS** is the operating system for a community.

It is not a new chat app. The group keeps living in WhatsApp, Discord, or Slack. This product is the **brain** of that group and the **front door** the world sees.

---

## What this is

A community already has years of messages, files, half-ideas, and “we should do a workshop.” None of that is organised. The lead cannot hold it in their head. Members are phone numbers. Events die in the thread.

Community OS **imports that habitat**, **saves everything**, and turns it into memory:

- **Community memory** — who we are, what we talk about, what we want next, what we’ve decided, what files we trust  
- **Member memory** — each person: name, what they do, what they want, what they can offer  

From that memory, the lead can **run the community**: see people clearly, notice demand (“people want an AI workshop”), match someone who can teach it with a room, and **publish a real event on our own pages** — with a QR code and RSVP. Guests stay in the same system as members.

Humans use a desk and public pages. Agents (Cursor, Grok) use the **same API**. The website is a client. The product is the infrastructure.

---

## Who it is for

**Community leads** — organisers who actually run the group. They are the customer.

**Members** — they keep chatting where they already are. They can RSVP on our event page and, if they choose, show a public ID card.

**Later:** venues and sponsors can sit on the same infrastructure. Not required for the product to exist.

---

## Problems it solves

**1. Nobody knows who is in the room**  
Leads know a thousand people as a phone number or a Discord handle. Chat dies because there are no intros, no memory of what people do, no way to find a speaker except asking around.

**2. The community has no memory**  
Decisions, docs, “I’ll host,” “we should do X” vanish in the scroll. Six months later you cannot find why you ran an event or who offered a venue.

**3. Wanting an event is not the same as having one**  
The group asks for a workshop. Someone could teach it. Somewhere could hold it. The lead still pieces that together by hand, then copies it into a page, then loses the guest list in another tool.

**4. There is no official face**  
Join links live in bios, Notion, and screenshots. There is no one URL or QR that says: this is the community, this is the next event, this is who I am in this room.

**5. Tools don’t share a brain**  
Vetting, intros, promo, venues, sponsors, newsletters — every job is a different tab. None of them know the same people. Agents cannot help because there is no API of the community, only a chat log.

**6. The last link is rented**  
If the event and the RSVPs live only on someone else’s platform, the lead still doesn’t own the path from conversation → guest. We keep that path **native**: chat → demand → proposal → **our event page** → **our RSVP**.

---

## Why it should exist

Communities are how people actually meet, learn, and ship — but they are run on **scrollback and goodwill**. Spreadsheets and Linktrees are not a brain. ChatGPT-on-an-export is not a brain: it forgets, it doesn’t cite, it doesn’t become an event page.

This should exist because:

- **The data already exists** in the chat. Someone should turn it into a durable, cited memory the lead can trust.  
- **Leads are drowning in admin** that is the same every week: who are they, what do they want, can we run this, who is coming.  
- **Members deserve a face** — a page and a badge — without being scraped into a public directory.  
- **Agents are useless** without a community API. Humans and machines should operate the same infrastructure, equally.  
- **The group should own the last mile.** The official event and the guest list should not depend on a third-party RSVP product.

If it works, a lead runs next week from the desk instead of from WhatsApp search. Another community can pick up the same system. That is why it deserves to exist: **communities are real institutions with no operating system.** This is that OS.

---

## What it is not

- Not a replacement for Discord, Slack, or WhatsApp  
- Not a feed, a social network, or a surveillance tool  
- Not “AI that reads every message live and talks for you”  
- Not a Luma clone — we are native on purpose  

AI helps **clean and draft**, on a schedule and a button. The product is **memory you can act on**.
